A History of 1049 Keith Avenue, Berkeley

Paul Theodore Beygrau (1871-1935) was a designer, artist, and inventor, born in England, who spent most of his life in North America. He was married to Marie Rosalie Schaaf, an Alsatian woodcarver.

By 1899, Beygrau was working in Seattle. His brother, Frederick, initiated a missing person inquiry for him there[*].

In 1914, Beygrau opened an Art School in Seattle. In promoting this[*], he claimed:

  • to have studied under the great masters of Art in England, France, Germany, and the Polish artist Stryowsky, the famous Genre painter of Prussia
  • to have done work for His Majesty, the King of England, Sir Wilfrid Laurier, Premier of Canada, Lord Minto, etc.
  • to come from a noted European family, his mother being a member of Lavellez de Montijaux (sic) family, and a near relative to the ex-Empress Eugenie of France.
  • that his brother was “head of the commercial science department of Columbia University” (see below)

Upon completion, 1049 Keith was prominently featured in local newspapers[*].

Paul's brother, Frederick Reginald Beygrau (1873-1941) was an author and educator on shorthand. He was Director of the Stenography and Typewriting Department at Columbia University's Teachers College (note how this was characterized above) and later taught it in New York high schools. Both brothers were friends of Luther Burbank (1849-1926), who died before 1049 Keith was complete. Burbank would visit while it was a work in progress and was the inspiration for the solarium: there were plants growing at the center of the circle of columns[*].

The Beygraus had taken out a mortgage to build on Keith, and in 1930, they lost their home to the bank and moved to Carmel.

Agnes Emily Hoyle (1883-1974), a San Francisco lawyer, bought 1049 Keith in foreclosure. She continued to live in San Francisco and initially only used it on weekends for parties. One, in particular, was an Italian themed dinner party, on Saturday, May 27, 1933, for the Queen's Bench, an organization of women lawyers[*].

Starting in 1936, Hoyle rented 1049 Keith to John W. Winkler (1894-1979), an Austrian born American etcher. He lived there until 1964, during which time the landscaping was improved with retaining walls and terraces. Winkler kept as pets a pair of skunks, who lived on the balcony, and a Gila monster named Montezuma. There are newspaper reports in the late '30s and 1940 of Montezuma escaping and wandering the neighborhood. [*]

Winkler[*] lived there with Elizabeth de Gebele Ginno (1907-1991), an artist born in England, but raised in Berkeley. By the '30s, Winkler was separated from his wife, Nora. They were eventually divorced in 1948, with Hoyle acting as his attorney, and John and Elizabeth married in 1949. In 1964, they moved to El Cerrito with their menagerie.

In 1958, Imogen Cunningham took a photo of Winkler in the solarium, which he used as a studio.

A photo by Cedric Wright from around 1936 shows Winkler carving boxes. The caption says it was taken in the basement at 1049 Keith, but, since there is no basement, it is not clear where it was taken.

By the late '60s, 1049 Keith was rented out to Berkeley graduate students, including David Minkus (1944-2024), a sociologist, Walter N Hittelman, now a cancer researcher, and his wife, Michele M Beaudoin. In December 1967, Judy “Gumbo” Clavir, a red diaper baby from Toronto, who had recently arrived in the Bay Area, moved in [*]. At a meeting about the Oakland 7, she met Stewart Edward “Stew” Albert (1939-2006), one of the founders of the Yippies. He soon joined her at 1049 Keith.

Albert was close friends with Eldridge Cleaver (1935-1998), Minister of Information for the Black Panthers. He and his wife, Kathleen, were frequent visitors to 1049 Keith. Supposedly this is where the Yippie Panther Pact, following the 1968 Democratic National Convention protests, was worked out.

Albert's reminiscences of 1049 Keith from his autobiography[*] are not altogether accurate, but give a sense of the impression it made.

Eldridge called out house the Hippie Palace. … Two Hollywood lesbians build the Hippie Palace in the late '20s. High in the Berkeley Hills they installed carved, stucco pillars and sunken Roman baths. Each room had its own fireplace with plastic angels. On a clear day you could see all of Berkeley, the Bay, and San Francisco from the front balcony.

By 1978, a restoration expert, needed to be called in for 1049 Keith. He repaired and recast the plaster, stripped paint off the doors, and rebuilt the columns in the solarium. It was then sold to and Egyptian-American doctor named Sobky (السبكي) and was then the Sobky family home for almost fifty years.

The April 25, 1982 Sunday supplement to the San Francisco Chronicle and the Examiner included a piece, titled “Quiet Elegance”, featuring unusual homes in the Berkeley Hills. One was 1049 Keith. This shows the restored columns, which have since been simplified. The supplement was in color, but, unfortunately, the scans I can find are not.

There are still remnants of when 1049 Keith was offered for sale in 2013, such as on Patch. This shows a few things that have since been undone:

  • The plaster drapes in the upstairs fireplace sculture are still painted, but not the cherubs.
  • The bathtup above the kitchen is still gold.
  • The vines and grapes in the downstairs fireplace scuplture are still painted, but not the two figures.
  • The solarium ceiling has some light painting.
  • The leaf pattern on the first floor ceiling is more extensive.

When 1049 Keith was again offered for sale in 2026, it was written up by local media, such as Sacramento Bee. And by specialized realtor sites, such as edificionado.

This being the 2020s, a number of realtors, besides just the listing agents, came and shot Instagram posts there, some of which give different perspectives on it: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7. It even got to the zillowgonewild Instagram and Reddit, where comments are a mix of love, hate, and odd projections of Berkeley life.

This being the later 2020s, there is what appears to be some kind of AI slop “reimagining” of it, that mashes up a few real images from the listing with architectural hallucinations. The cui bono of this is not at all clear to me.

1049 Keith sold to a seventy-year-old couple: Joyce, an entrepreneur who runs a tall ship charter business on the San Francisco Bay, and Mike, an MIT hacker who works at Apple. They hope to complement it with their art collection, assembled over the nearly half century that they have been together.
